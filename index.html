    About us

    A small charity with 20 years of local partnerships

    Educator Michelle Bonneau founded Her International in 2005. She sold her home in Kelowna and went to Nepal at 58, at the height of the Maoist insurgency. She began working with some of the most marginalized women and girls there, including girls in bonded labour. In 2017 she received the Meritorious Service Medal for this work.

    Today a volunteer board of directors in Kelowna runs Her International. We work hand in hand with Creating Possibilities Nepal, a locally led organization we've partnered with since 2008. Its staff lead the programs on the ground, and we raise the funding and support.

    We don't choose scholarship recipients. Local Mothers' Groups, who know their communities best, identify the families most in need.

    Who you support

    Students

    Girls in the Dang region of Nepal

    Many come from Indigenous Tharu families whose daughters were once sold into bonded labour. The practice is now illegal, but extreme poverty still keeps girls out of school.

    • 255 students supported this year
    • From primary school to post-secondary and technical studies
    • Regular school and home visits, plus counselling
    Women

    Mothers building financial independence

    Mothers' Groups meet every month to save, lend and learn together. These groups keep their daughters' education going for the long term.

    • 1,965 women in 66 groups
    • Microcredit, savings and financial literacy
    • Leadership and public-speaking workshops
    Youth

    Teens becoming leaders

    Young people aged 13 to 18 learn communication, decision-making, entrepreneurship and goal-setting through weekly hands-on sessions.

    • 86 youth in We Create this year
    • 578 girls through She Creates to date
    • Workshops for girls in Kelowna, too

    Our programs

    Six programs, one mission

    She Learns: Scholarships

    Covers everything a girl needs to stay in school for a year: fees, tutoring, uniform, shoes, school bag, supplies and hygiene products.

    Bicycles

    Students who show commitment to school get a bike between Grades 7 and 9. That means safer travel, more time to study and a visible sign that she's a student. 313 bikes so far.

    She Thrives: Mothers' Groups

    Life-skills and microcredit groups where mothers become self-reliant, personally and financially. One mother took a loan to become the area's first female auto-rickshaw driver.

    We Create: Youth Leadership

    Weekly sessions on leadership, financial literacy, entrepreneurship and goal-setting for teens aged 13 to 18.

    Her Health

    Menstrual and reproductive health education with UBC and Saalt. More than 2,500 women and girls took part in workshops this year.

    Builds

    Unako House community centre and classrooms where they're needed, giving students safe places to learn. Seven builds so far, reaching 23,000 people a year.

            Questions donors ask

            Frequently asked

            Will I get a tax receipt?

            Yes. Her International is a registered Canadian charity (#). US donors can give through The Safe Harbor Foundation, a registered 501(c)(3), and get a US tax receipt.

            How are scholarship students chosen?

            Local Mothers' Groups identify the families most in need. Creating Possibilities Nepal then works with local officials to welcome those students into the program. Staff visit each student's school and home, track her progress and offer counselling.

            What does $33 a month cover?

            One girl's full school year: admission and exam fees, group tutoring, school uniform, house dress, shoes and socks, school bag, books and supplies, hygiene products (toothbrush, soap, sanitary napkins and so on), and a bicycle once she meets the requirements.

            What do programs, administration and fundraising mean?

            These are the three categories in our annual financial overview. In 2025–26 we spent on programs, on administration and on fundraising. The full breakdown is in each year's report.

            Are there other ways to give?

            Yes. You can mail a cheque, donate securities or cryptocurrency, plan a legacy gift, join the annual Walk for Her, or join the Nepal Build trip, which runs April 3 to 18, 2027.

            Choose a gift, or give any amount. Monthly donors give students a full year of steady support.
